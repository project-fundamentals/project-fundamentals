How banks actually work

Where a bank's profit really comes from.

By the end of this page

  • Where a bank's profit actually comes from
  • Why savings rates are low and card rates are high — on purpose
  • How to choose between a savings account and a fixed deposit

Banks are not just a safe place to store money. DBS, OCBC and UOB are among the most profitable companies in Singapore — and knowing how that business makes its money explains almost everything about how they treat yours.

Where a bank's profit comes from

Banks earn money primarily by charging interest on the loans they give out. Deposits from customers are what fund those loans.

In simple terms the gap between the interest a bank pays you on savings and the interest it charges borrowers is the core of the business. Real banking involves regulatory capital requirements, fees, and other income sources too — this is the simplified version worth knowing first.

This is why a savings account pays a low rate while a credit card charges a high one. Both numbers are set by the same institution, and the difference between them is the point.

Every loan, credit card balance, or overdraft that is not paid off in full is generating income for the bank — not just providing a convenience for you.

What this means for you

  • Your deposits are not idle. They are being lent out. This is normal and regulated, and it is also why banks want you to keep money with them.
  • Interest rates are a two-sided story. A rate that is good for a saver is bad for a borrower, and vice versa.
  • "Free" services usually are not. If an account or card costs nothing directly, the bank is earning from it somewhere else.

Savings accounts versus fixed deposits

A regular savings account gives you full access to your money at any time, and pays very little for that flexibility. A fixed deposit pays more because you commit to leaving the money alone for a set period — the bank can plan around it.

Neither is better in general. Which one fits depends entirely on whether you might need the money soon.
