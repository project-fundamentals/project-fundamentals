Credit cards & the debt trap

Compound interest and the minimum payment trap.

By the end of this page

  • How compound interest makes unpaid debt grow on itself
  • Why minimum payments keep you in debt far longer than expected
  • Try it yourself: an interactive minimum-payment calculator

A credit card is a short-term loan you take out every time you use it. If the balance is cleared in full each month, that loan is free. If it is not, it becomes one of the most expensive forms of borrowing available.

How debt accumulates

The risk is that debt can accumulate quickly if balances are not fully repaid. Two mechanisms do the damage:

  • Compound interest — interest is charged on the balance, then next month on the balance plus the interest already added. Debt grows on itself.
  • Minimum payment traps — paying only the minimum each month feels responsible, but it barely touches the principal and dramatically increases the total you eventually pay.

The takeaway

Understanding these mechanics now helps you spend responsibly, avoid debt, and manage money wisely later — at the point where credit cards actually become available to you, rather than after the first statement arrives.
