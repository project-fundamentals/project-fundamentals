Low-risk investments

SSBs, fixed deposits and T-bills. Safe and steady.

By the end of this page

  • How SSBs, fixed deposits and T-bills actually work
  • What 'capital protected' really means — and what it costs you
  • Which option fits pocket-money-scale savings

Category 1 — Safe & steady

These are the options where your capital is protected. Returns are modest, and that is the deal you are accepting in exchange for near-certainty.

Singapore Savings Bonds (SSBs)

100% backed by the SG Government

What it is: A long-term bond fully backed by the Singapore Government, meaning your initial capital is completely safe.

How it works: You lend money to the government, and they pay you interest every 6 months. The longer you hold the bond — up to 10 years — the higher the interest rate becomes.

Reality check: You can withdraw your money in any given month with no penalty, but the returns are modest compared to riskier options. Minimum investment is $500.

Even if the stock market crashes tomorrow, your SSB money is completely untouched and guaranteed by law.

Great for saving up for big future goals — like a university fund — without risking your pocket money.

Fixed deposits

Guaranteed returns, zero surprises

What it is: An arrangement with a bank where you lock away a specific sum for a fixed period — 6 months, a year — at a guaranteed interest rate.

How it works: The bank rewards you with a higher interest rate than a regular savings account because you promise not to touch that money until the maturity date.

Reality check: If you withdraw early due to an emergency, you lose most or all of the accumulated interest. Ideal only for money you are certain you will not need soon.

Banks pay higher interest here because you are making a promise not to touch that cash for a set period. It is a pact between you and the bank.

Use this for money you know you will not need for school expenses or outings over the next year.

Treasury bills (T-bills)

Short-term safety net

What it is: Short-term government debt issued by the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), maturing in either 6 months or 1 year.

How it works: They are bought at a discount rather than paying regular interest.

  1. 1 You buy the bill at a discount — e.g. $980
  2. 2 You wait 6 or 12 months
  3. 3 Government pays back face value — $1,000

Reality check: Your money is locked for the full duration, and the minimum investment is $1,000 — a high barrier on secondary school pocket money.

T-bills do not pay regular interest payouts. Your profit is baked right into the discount price you pay on day one.

Highly secure, but with a $1,000 entry it is a goal to save toward rather than something you buy with one week's allowance.
