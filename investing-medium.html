Investing

Medium-risk investments

Blue-chip stocks, ETFs and index funds. Market growth.

What you'll learn

  • What owning a stock actually means
  • How ETFs spread risk across hundreds of companies at once
  • What diversification protects you from — and what it can't

Category 2 — Market growth

Here your capital is genuinely at risk, and prices move daily. In exchange, these are the options with a realistic history of growing money meaningfully over long periods.

Blue-chip stocks

Own a piece of a giant

What it is: Shares of ownership in large, well-established, financially stable companies with a history of reliable performance — Singtel, DBS, or Apple, for example.

How it works: When you buy a stock you own a tiny piece of the company. If it makes a profit and grows, your stock value goes up, and it may share profits with you through dividends.

Reality check: Prices fluctuate daily based on market news, company earnings, and the economy. Safer than small startup stocks, but you can still lose money if the company encounters structural issues.

When these giant companies make huge profits, they often distribute a portion of that cash directly back to their shareholders. Those are dividends.

Daily prices will wobble, but these companies have strong foundations to survive economic storms over the long haul.

Exchange-traded funds (ETFs)

Don't put all your eggs in one basket

What it is: A basket of different stocks bundled together into a single fund that trades on the stock market, letting you buy into hundreds of companies at once.

How it works: Instead of putting all your money into one company, an ETF spreads it across many — the top 30 companies in Singapore, or the top 500 in the US. This is called diversification.

One company crashes. The basket holds.

  • ▼Falling

Reality check: Diversification protects you from a single company going bankrupt, but it does not protect you from a general market crash. If the entire market goes down, your ETF drops too.

Buying an ETF is like buying a sampler platter at a restaurant instead of committing to one single dish. It instantly diversifies your risk.

The ultimate "set-and-forget" market option for beginners who do not want to stress over tracking individual company news.

S&P 500 index funds

The standard benchmark for market growth

What it is: A fund tracking the 500 largest publicly traded corporations in America, spread across tech, healthcare, finance and retail.

How it works: A single investment is instantly divided into fractional pieces of 500 different massive companies, rather than betting on any one of them.

Historically the S&P 500 has been considered one of the best benchmarks for long-term wealth creation, averaging a return of around 10% per year over many decades. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

Instead of trying to guess which individual company will win, you are investing in the growth of the entire US economy. It still wobbles day to day.
