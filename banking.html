Banking & Digital Payments

Tap-to-pay is convenient. That's exactly the problem.

Understanding how banks, credit, and digital payments actually work now helps you avoid debt and spend responsibly later.

Where a bank's money comes from

Banks aren't just a safe place to store money — they're a business. Banks earn money by charging interest on the loans they give out. Every loan, credit card balance, or overdraft that isn't paid off in full is generating income for the bank, not just a convenience for you.

Why tapping doesn't feel like spending

Credit cards and digital payments — e-wallets, contactless, online checkout — separate the act of spending from the act of paying. That separation is convenient, but it can encourage overspending, because the moment of paying no longer lines up with the moment of buying.

Risk: if a credit card balance isn't fully repaid, debt can accumulate quickly.

The minimum payment trap

Paying only the minimum on a balance feels responsible — you're paying something. But compound interest means unpaid debt grows over time, and minimum payments can keep you paying for far longer than expected.
