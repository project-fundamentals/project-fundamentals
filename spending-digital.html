Why tapping doesn't feel like spending

Digital payments and the disappearing pain of paying.

What you'll learn

  • What the 'pain of paying' is and why tapping removes it
  • Why small digital payments become invisible until totalled
  • Four practical ways to put the friction back

Paying with cash makes losing money physical and immediate. You watch the notes leave your wallet, and you can see what is left. Tapping a card or a phone removes that friction entirely — and removing friction changes behaviour.

The pain of paying

Economists call the small psychological sting of handing over money the pain of paying. It is uncomfortable by design, and that discomfort is useful: it acts as a natural brake on spending.

Digital payments reduce that pain almost to zero. There is no physical loss to observe, no dwindling stack of notes, and often no moment where you consciously register a number at all. The purchase completes before the brake has a chance to engage.

Spending becomes invisible

The result is that money leaves without being noticed. Think about a normal school day in Singapore: tap in at the MRT gantry with SimplyGo, PayLah! a friend back for lunch, tap your card at the kopitiam drinks stall, GrabPay for the bubble tea after school. Not one of those moments involved seeing money — and by dinner, most people cannot say what the day cost.

This is not an argument for going back to cash. Digital payments are convenient, safer to carry, and here to stay. The point is that the natural brake is gone, so you have to supply a deliberate one.

Putting the friction back

  • Check your balance before you buy, not after. This restores the moment of awareness that cash used to force.
  • Use one account for spending. Splitting spending money from savings makes the limit visible.
  • Review your transaction history weekly. The DBS PayLah!, OCBC and SimplyGo apps all list every tap. Totalling one week of small payments is usually the moment it clicks.
  • Turn off saved card details for online shops you use often, so each purchase takes a deliberate step.
