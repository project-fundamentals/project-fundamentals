Spending

Why we overspend

Impulse buying, instant gratification, and social pressure.

What you'll learn

  • Why overspending is a psychology problem, not a knowledge problem
  • The three forces working against you: instant gratification, peer pressure, invisible costs
  • A 4-question self-check to run before any purchase

Teenagers often overspend despite already knowing the basic financial advice. That gap between knowing and doing is the whole point: overspending is rarely a knowledge problem. It is a psychology problem, and the pressures behind it are predictable enough to plan around.

Instant gratification beats long-term thinking

Given a choice between something good now and something better later, the teenage brain reliably over-weights now. This is not a character flaw. It is how the brain weighs an immediate reward against a delayed one, and it is the same mechanism that makes revision harder than scrolling.

The practical consequence is that long-term costs get underestimated. Watch what one small habit does over a school year:

The trick is not willpower. It is putting a gap between the impulse and the payment — even a few minutes is usually enough for the urgency to fade.

Social pressure and peer influence

Purchases do not happen in a vacuum. What your friends own, what is trending online, and the fear of being left out all quietly raise the bar for what counts as a normal thing to buy — even when nothing about the item itself has changed.

This is why the same shoes can feel essential in one month and irrelevant the next. The shoes did not change. The social context did.

People are not rational about money

Behavioural economics is the study of exactly this: people do not always make rational financial decisions, even when they have all the information. Recognising the specific patterns that affect you is more useful than resolving to be more disciplined in general.

  • Prioritising instant gratification — the reward now outweighs the larger reward later.
  • Underestimating long-term costs — small repeated spending is invisible until it is totalled up.
  • Being influenced by peers and online trends — wants get manufactured by context.
